A small change that lasts is easy to miss when every day bounces around more than the change itself: sleep a little shorter, a reading a little higher. Page’s cumulative-sum test (CUSUM, 1954) adds up how far each number sits above (or below) the usual level, minus an allowance for ordinary noise, and raises a flag once the running total gets too big for chance. It is not magic: on made-up series of 60 numbers this page flags about 1 flat series in 11 by mistake and catches about half of shifts as small as 0.6 of the day-to-day spread (bigger shifts more often). It is a standard tool in quality control. Paste your own numbers (one per line, or separated by commas) or try a made-up series with a hidden shift. The page says whether there is a lasting shift, which way, and about where it began. Whether this catches a change in your own numbers before you would have noticed it yourself is an open question.
