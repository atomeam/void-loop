the narrow slice · draft · not published

Each round shows a cloud of points with its correlation, then shades a band: only the points whose x falls in it. Before you see the band's own correlation, guess whether it is stronger, about the same, or weaker. Looking only inside a narrow range of x usually makes the link look weaker than it is (range restriction: Thorndike, 1949; Sackett and Yang, 2000), which is why, for example, test scores can look useless among people who were all admitted on those scores. This page counts how often you see it coming. A hypothesis about your own reading of charts, nothing more. Everything stays in this browser.
