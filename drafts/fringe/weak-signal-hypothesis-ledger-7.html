A sign shows up: a test comes back positive, an alarm goes off, a hunch feels strong. How likely is the thing behind it, really? The answer leans heavily on how rare the thing is to begin with, and people tend to skip that part (Kahneman and Tversky, 1973). Put the same facts as counts of people instead of percentages and far more people get it right (Gigerenzer and Hoffrage, 1995). Each round gives you a case in percentages. Guess, then see it as counts out of 1,000. The page keeps how far off your guesses are, so you can see whether that habit shrinks. Everything stays in this browser.
