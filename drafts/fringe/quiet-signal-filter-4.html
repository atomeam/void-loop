noise that helps · draft · not published

Each round hides a faint bar, either upright or lying flat, in a small grid of black and white squares. A square turns white only when the bar's faint light plus some random noise crosses a fixed threshold. With no noise the bar is too faint to cross it and the grid stays black; with a lot of noise the bar drowns. Stochastic resonance says a middle amount of noise lets the most of the bar through (Gammaitoni and others, 1998; Simonotto and others, 1997, saw it in people looking at pictures like these). Say which way the bar lies; this page counts how often you are right at each noise level. It is a hypothesis to test on your own eyes. Everything stays in this browser.
