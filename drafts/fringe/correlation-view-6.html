the link turns over · draft · not published

Three made-up groups of points. Inside every group, more of x goes with more of y. Pool them and the overall line can slope the other way, because the groups sit in different places: the groups with more x happen to start lower. This is Simpson's paradox (Simpson 1951; the Berkeley admissions case in Bickel, Hammel and O'Connell 1975 is the famous real one). Slide the gap between the groups and watch the pooled line turn over while every group's own line keeps its slope. Whether seeing the groups split out changes how a person reads a pooled number of their own is an open question this page asks. Nothing is saved.
