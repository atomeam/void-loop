Ten dots drift for four seconds. In about half the rounds one dot is chasing another; in the rest none is. After each round say which it was. The chaser does not always head straight at its target: it may wander up to some angle off the direct line (the "chasing subtlety"). Gao, Newman and Scholl (2009) found people spot a chase easily when the chaser heads straight at its target and barely above chance once it can wander 90° off; Heider and Simmel (1944) showed people read intent into plain moving shapes. This page counts how often you are right at each angle. It is a hypothesis about your own eyes, nothing more. Everything stays in this browser.
