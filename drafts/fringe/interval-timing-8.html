The lamp lights for a while, then goes out. Then press and hold the space bar (or the hold button) for as long as it was lit. The lamp shows three lengths, mixed up. Most people are about as far off in proportion at every length: twice as long a time, about twice as wide a spread. That is the scalar property of timing (Gibbon’s scalar expectancy theory, 1977, and many studies since; Grondin’s 2010 review). This page works out your own spread as a share of the time (spread ÷ average) at each length, so you can see whether yours stays level. Whether your level is steady from one sitting to the next is an open question this page asks, not a finding. Everything stays in this browser.
