ten looks at noise · draft · not published

Each "study" makes up two groups of 20 people and measures ten things about them. Every number is random: the groups do not differ in anything. The page then tests each of the ten for a difference, the usual way, and marks any result below p = 0.05 as a "finding". With one look, a false finding turns up 5% of the time. With ten looks it turns up about 40% of the time (1 − 0.95¹⁰), which is how checking many things and reporting the one that worked fills journals with effects that are not there (Simmons, Nelson and Simonsohn, 2011). Tick the box to demand p < 0.005 instead, one tenth of 0.05 for ten looks (the Bonferroni correction), and watch the false findings drop back to about 5%. Keep a weak signal in the ledger as a hypothesis until it survives the looks it took to find it. Everything stays in this browser.
