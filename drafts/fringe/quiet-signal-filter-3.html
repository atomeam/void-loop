a tone in the hiss · draft · not published

Each trial is one second of hiss. In half of them a faint tone is hidden in it. Say whether you heard a tone. The page then separates two things people usually mix up: how well you can hear the tone (sensitivity, d′) and how ready you are to say "yes" (your bias). Someone who says yes to everything catches every tone but also "hears" tones that are not there; this measure is not fooled by that. It comes from signal detection theory (Green and Swets, 1966), used for radar, medical scans and hearing tests. The tone gets fainter as you do well and louder when you miss.
