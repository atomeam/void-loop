how small a button · draft · not published

Tap the blue dot, then the yellow one as fast as you can. The yellow dots come in different sizes and distances. The time a pointing move takes grows with how hard the target is, measured as log2(distance ÷ size + 1) bits; that straight line is Fitts's law (Fitts 1954; MacKenzie 1992 for its use in interface design). This page fits your own line from your own taps, then says how small a button can get, at a usual reach, before it costs you more than a tenth of a second. Whether sizing a gesture or a button to one person's own line makes it easier for them than a one-size rule is an open question this page asks. Nothing leaves this browser and nothing is saved.
