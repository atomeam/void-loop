the pointing arrow · draft · not published

An arrow in the middle points left or right, then a star appears in one of the two boxes: press F for left or J for right (or tap the boxes) as fast as you can. Most of the time the arrow is right; sometimes it lies. People answer faster when the arrow was right and slower when it lied; Posner described this cueing effect in 1980 and it is one of the standard ways to measure where attention is pointed. The gap between the two is your own cost of having attention sent to the wrong place. This page keeps it per sitting with the hour of day, so over time you can see whether a misleading pointer costs you more at some times than others. That last part is an open question this page asks; it is not an established finding. Everything stays in this browser.
