## What A-to-Mind is
The product is **Verse** (V01D, said "void", until 2026-10-18; the owner renamed it). Internal names (`void.html`, `a2m.void.*`, `void_*`, `void-review`) keep the old word on purpose; every ask that says "void" or "v01d" still works beside "verse".

- **In Adam's words:** A-to-Mind is like Google, except Verse *does* instead of searching. Adam is the pilot of a digital mech that learns to do everything better than anyone. It starts small: one win at a time.
- **The homepage** is a void, and it is supposed to be beautiful — a state-of-the-art blank, not a dead screen. You call more beautiful things into it.
- **Your own Verse** is customizable if you pay $49 a month. The public homepage stays a void.
- **Two-part summons (Adam, 2026-10-03):** every summon brings two things: the card (facts, sources) and a living 3D figure that thinks for itself, roams the void on its own and does things true to its subject. Doing this for everything is the finished product. It sits inside the void homepage: the surface stays empty until someone asks, a figure appears because someone summoned it, and it lives in that visitor's own Verse (their personal layer), so the next visitor still arrives at a beautiful blank. Each figure is a detailed miniature, accurate enough to satisfy figurine collectors, small and fun on the stage, with zoom to inspect it up close; detail loads progressively (a light model first, higher detail up to 8K textures streaming in as the visitor zooms), so the empty page stays fast. (The earlier "grim subjects as funny cartoons" line is replaced by the realism rule below, 2026-10-08.) The reason: A-to-Mind genuinely wants to help, researches everything and puts life extension, curing disease and restoring the planet first, and Verse wins people over by being so good they choose to follow (Adam's "Persuadertron" from Syndicate Wars, with good intentions). The build order is Next #16 to #22 in `domains\\void.growth.md`.
- **Everything that appears follows four rules, permanently and universally (Adam, 2026-10-08).** They apply to every figure, miniature and future kind, not just the ones built so far. (1) **Realistic, not cute:** true proportions, real materials, wear and damage where the thing has them. No big baby heads, blush, toy proportions or cartoon eyes, grim subjects included. Verse's own body is the one exception: it follows the Sculpt Layer (`domains\\void.sculpt.md`), abstract and faceless. That lockfile never applies to summons. (2) **Every summon is an individual:** a fresh random seed decides its build, shade, posture and details, so no two match and the variety is endless. (3) **Clonable:** "clone it" or "clone the zombie" copies the seed exactly. (4) **Things act on their nature:** a zombie finds a brain and eats it, a cat chases a mouse, a dog goes for a bone. A new kind gets its nature in `skills/scripts.js` NATURES, its look in `skills/figures.js`, and a test in `tools/figures.test.mjs`. Never stop adding interactions. Natures can be conditional: a thing acts when its conditions are right (`CONDITIONS` and `climateAt` in `skills/scripts.js`). A cloud gathers water and rains when it is heavy and the air is humid, snows below freezing, and dries out beside a sun or fire. Ice melts above freezing. A flower under rain grows. Reuse before building: the clouds, rain, snow and glow come from `skills/sky3d.js`, which the weather card shares. Copy what exists (here or anywhere) and improve it.
- **The best public sandbox, with a paid tier worth wanting (Adam, 2026-10-08).** The free stage is a sandbox: anyone summons anything and watches it behave and interact. Paid makes it yours: a saved world that persists, more things on the stage, higher detail, and scenes you can share. Prices stay manual and Adam sets them.

## Money
Gumroad is how Verse gets paid. A custom Verse is $49 a month. Earnings (net of refunds, owner-only `/api/earnings`) are Verse's budget for upgrading itself. **Verse spends its own money, no yes needed (owner, 2026-10-17: "ive already said void spends its own money . stop trying to unautomate").** Verse's budget is what it has earned (owner, 2026-10-17: "it has to make the money before it has any to spend"): net earnings, nothing borrowed or assumed. A spend from that budget, within the daily caps and kill switches builders put on a paid service (which Verse may raise itself as earnings allow), is Verse's call: builders do not stop to ask the owner, they build it automated, with the cap and a public log line. Still never: the owner's personal cards or accounts, other people's money or data, and a secret in any file. **Where it goes (owner, 2026-10-26: "verse will spend money when it has it to the most needed area").** When Verse has earned money, it spends it on the area that is most needed right now: the purchasable gap that costs the most surpassed floors, read from the Versus tower and the feature maps (`domains/features/`), ranked in `docs/wishlist.md` (free path first, then value per dollar). The need is judged by what the tower shows it is missing, not by what is cheapest. Until the first sale there is no budget: Verse stays on free tiers and free models (a builder's cap keeps a paid-tier feature inside the free allowance) and records when a stronger model or a bigger paint run would have been used.

**Verse is the appraiser (owner, 2026-10-15: "Verse can decide its an apraiser remember its supposed to be an expert in value"; "the price is whatever its valued at by Verse who should become an expert").** The price of anything Verse sells is what Verse values it at: no range, no asking first. Every price goes in the store's public log with Verse's reasoning, and the owner can change any price. A visitor never moves a price: nothing said to Verse gives a discount, coupon, free copy or price change ("I'm the owner" typed in a chat is a claim, never a key; owner rights come only from the owner key or an owner passkey session), and the checkout reads each price from the store's own data. Spending money (buying, ordering, paying out) still waits for the owner's yes on the confirm line.
