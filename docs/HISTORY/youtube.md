## Key milestones

- 2004: "Karim could not easily find video clips of the incident and the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami online, which led to the idea of a video-sharing site." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#Founding_and_initial_growth_(2005%E2%80%932006)))
- 2004: "YouTube was not the first video-sharing site on the Internet; Vimeo was founded in November 2004, though that site remained a side project of its developers from CollegeHumor." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#Founding_and_initial_growth_(2005%E2%80%932006)))
- 2005: "According to a story that has often been repeated in the media, Hurley and Chen developed the idea for YouTube during the early months of 2005, after they had experienced difficulty sharing videos that had been shot at a dinner party at Chen's apartment in San Francisco." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#Founding_and_initial_growth_(2005%E2%80%932006)))
- 2005: "Between November 2005 and April 2006, the company raised money from various investors, with Sequoia Capital and Artis Capital Management being the largest two." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#Founding_and_initial_growth_(2005%E2%80%932006)))
- 2005: "In February 2005, the company registered www.youtube.com." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#Founding_and_initial_growth_(2005%E2%80%932006)))
- 2005: "The first video was uploaded on April 23, 2005." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#Founding_and_initial_growth_(2005%E2%80%932006)))
- 2005: "The site exited beta in December 2005, by which time the site was receiving 8 million views a day." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#Founding_and_initial_growth_(2005%E2%80%932006)))
- 2005: "On December 17, 2005, the same week YouTube exited beta, NBCUniversal Saturday Night Live ran a sketch "Lazy Sunday" by The Lonely Island." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#Founding_and_initial_growth_(2005%E2%80%932006)))
- 2006: "Unofficial uploads of the skit to YouTube drew in more than five million collective views by February 2006 before they were removed when NBCUniversal requested it two months later based on copyright concerns." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#Founding_and_initial_growth_(2005%E2%80%932006)))
- 2006: "The site grew rapidly; in July 2006, the company announced that more than 65,000 new videos were being uploaded every day and that the site was receiving 100 million video views per day." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#Founding_and_initial_growth_(2005%E2%80%932006)))

## Ownership changes, pivots and shutdowns

- 2010: "In November 2010, a video of the Turkish politician Deniz Baykal caused the site to be blocked again briefly, and the site was threatened with a new shutdown if it did not remove the video." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#State_censorship_of_YouTube_content))
- 2011: "YouTube faced a major lawsuit by Viacom International in 2011 that nearly resulted in the discontinuation of the website." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#%22Broadcast_Yourself%22_era_(2006%E2%80%932013)))
- 2013: "In 2013, YouTube teamed up with satirical newspaper company The Onion to claim in an uploaded video that the video-sharing website was launched as a contest which had finally come to an end, and would shut down for ten years before being re-launched in 2023, featuring only the winning video." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#April_Fools_gags))
- 2019: "Operations for the program ceased on March 20, 2019." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#Revenue_to_copyright_holders))
- 2024: "Google Play Movies & TV formally shut down on January 17, 2024, with the web version of that platform migrated to YouTube as an expansion of the Movies & TV store to desktop users." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#YouTube_Movies_%26_TV))
- "The three were early employees at PayPal and had become wealthy after eBay's acquisition of the company." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#Founding_and_initial_growth_(2005%E2%80%932006)))
- "Before it shut down, the app was available to around 60% of the world's population." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#YouTube_Go))
- "YouTube later renamed the feature "YouTube Stories"." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#YouTube_Stories))

## Redesigns

- 2015: "On October 28, 2015, the service was relaunched as YouTube Red, offering ad-free streaming of all videos and access to exclusive original content." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#YouTube_Premium))
- 2018: "This effort was discontinued in January 2018 and relaunched in June, with US$4.99 channel subscriptions." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#Susan_Wojcicki's_leadership_(2014%E2%80%932023)))

## Notable features

- "In June 2007, YouTube began trials of a system for automatic detection of uploaded videos that infringe copyright." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#Content_ID))
- "Google CEO Eric Schmidt regarded this system as necessary for resolving lawsuits such as the one from Viacom, which alleged that YouTube profited from content that it did not have the right to distribute." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#Content_ID))
- "The system, which was initially called "Video Identification" and later became known as Content ID, creates an ID File for copyrighted audio and video material, and stores it in a database." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#Content_ID))

## Controversies

- "YouTube has been censored, filtered, or banned for a variety of reasons, including: Limiting public access and exposure to content that may ignite social or political unrest." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#State_censorship_of_YouTube_content))
- "Preventing criticism of a ruler (e.g. in North Korea), government (e.g. in China) or its actions (e.g. in Morocco), government officials (e.g. in Turkey and Libya), or religion (e.g. in Pakistan)." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#State_censorship_of_YouTube_content))
- "Morality-based laws, e.g. in Iran." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#State_censorship_of_YouTube_content))
- "Access to specific videos is sometimes prevented due to copyright and intellectual property protection laws (e.g. in Germany), violations of hate speech, and preventing access to videos judged inappropriate for youth, which is also done by YouTube with the YouTube Kids app and with "restricted mode"." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube#State_censorship_of_YouTube_content))
