## Key milestones

- 2011: "In September 2011 Wired magazine named the application one of "The 7 Coolest Startups You Haven't Heard of Yet"." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trello#History))
- 2014: "In 2014, it raised US$10.3 million in funding from Index Ventures and Spark Capital." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trello#History))
- 2015: "In May 2015, Trello expanded internationally with localized interfaces for Brazil, Germany, and Spain." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trello#History))
- 2016: "In May 2016, Trello claimed it had more than 1.1 million daily active users and 14 million total signups." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trello#History))
- 2016: "In 2016 Trello launched the Power-Up platform, allowing 3rd party developers to build and distribute extensions known as Power-Ups to Trello." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trello#History))
- 2017: "On 9 January 2017, Atlassian announced its intent to acquire Trello for $425 million." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trello#History))
- 2019: "Trello announced 35 million users in March 2019 and 50 million users in October 2019." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trello#History))
- 2020: "In 2020 Craig Jones, then cybersecurity operations director at Sophos, found that the company exposed the personally identifiable information (PII) data of its users, exposed through public Trello boards; the researcher first tweeted about this issue in the year 2018." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trello#History))
- 2022: "By January 2022 there were a total of 247 power-ups listed in the Power-Up directory." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trello#History))
- 2024: "On 16 January 2024 Trello suffered a data breach containing over 15 million unique email addresses, names and usernames, when the data was posted on a popular hacking forum." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trello#History))
