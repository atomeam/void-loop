## Controversies

- "In 2022, Google began accepting requests for the removal of phone numbers, physical addresses and email addresses from its search results." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Search#Privacy))
- "It had previously accepted requests for removing confidential data only, such as Social Security numbers, bank account and credit card numbers, personal signatures, and medical records." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Search#Privacy))
- "Even with the new policy, Google may remove information from only certain but not all search queries." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Search#Privacy))
- "It would not remove content that is "broadly useful", such as news articles, or already part of the public record." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Search#Privacy))
- "In 2012, the US Federal Trade Commission fined Google US$22.5 million for violating their agreement not to violate the privacy of users of Apple's Safari web browser." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Search#Privacy))
- "The FTC was also continuing to investigate if Google's favoring of their own services in their search results violated antitrust regulations." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Search#Privacy))
