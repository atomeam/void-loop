## Ownership changes, pivots and shutdowns

- 1977: "NPR was primarily a production and distribution organization until 1977, when it merged with the Association of Public Radio Stations." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR#1970s))
- 1999: "NPR's history in digital media includes the work of an independent, for-profit company called Public Interactive, which was founded in 1999 and acquired by PRI in June 2004, when it became a non-profit company." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR#Digital_media))
- 2018: "In May 2018, a group led by NPR acquired the podcasting app Pocket Casts." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR#Digital_media))
- 2018: "In April 2018, the NPR app (renamed from the NPR News app), underwent a significant update that changed the design, user experience, selection of content, and added breaking news notifications." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR#Mobile_Apps))
- 2018: "American Public Media (APM) and Public Radio Exchange (PRX; which also merged with Public Radio International in 2018) are other major public radio production and distribution organizations with distinct missions, and each competes with the other and NPR for programming slots on public radio stations." ## Controversies

- "On April 5, 2023, following Elon Musk's acquisition of the American social media platform Twitter, NPR's main Twitter account was designated as "US state-affiliated media"." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR#Elon_Musk_%2F_Twitter_controversy))
- "This label was typically reserved for foreign media outlets that directly represented the point of view of their respective governments, like Russia's RT and China's Xinhua." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR#Elon_Musk_%2F_Twitter_controversy))
- "Twitter's designation was widely considered controversial as NPR is an independent news organization that receives only a tiny fraction of its funding from the government." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR#Elon_Musk_%2F_Twitter_controversy))
- "Twitter's previous policy had explicitly mentioned NPR, as well as the United Kingdom's BBC, as examples of networks that were not considered as state-affiliated due to their editorial independence." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR#Elon_Musk_%2F_Twitter_controversy))
