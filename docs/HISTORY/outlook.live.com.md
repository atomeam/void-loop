## Key milestones

- 2011: "It was changed back to Hotmail in October 2011 and was fully replaced by Outlook in May 2013, sharing the same brand as the Microsoft Outlook software which is offered via a Microsoft 365 (formerly Microsoft Office) subscription." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlook.com))

## Ownership changes, pivots and shutdowns

- 1995: "Founded in 1995 by Sabeer Bhatia and Jack Smith as Hotmail, it was acquired by Microsoft in 1997 for an estimated $400 million, with it becoming part of the MSN family of online services, branded as MSN Hotmail." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlook.com))
- 1997: "Hotmail was sold to Microsoft in December 1997 for a reported $400 million (~$744 million in 2025), and it joined the MSN group of services." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlook.com#MSN_Hotmail))
- 2007: "In May 2007, the service was rebranded to Windows Live Hotmail, as part of the Windows Live suite of products." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlook.com))
- 2009: "Outlook.com supports email clients connecting through the following protocols, listed in chronological order: WebDAV was used by Outlook Express but was discontinued on September 1, 2009." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlook.com#Mail_client_access))

## Redesigns

## Controversies

- "In 1999, hackers revealed a security flaw in Hotmail that permitted anybody to log in to any Hotmail account using the password 'eh'." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlook.com#Security_issues))
- "At the time, it was called "the most widespread security incident in the history of the Web"." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlook.com#Security_issues))
- "In 2001, the Hotmail service was compromised again by computer hackers who discovered that anyone could log in to their Hotmail account and then pull messages from any other Hotmail account by crafting a URL with the second account's username and a valid message number." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlook.com#Security_issues))
- "It was such a simple attack that by the time the patch was made, dozens of newspapers and hundreds of web sites published exact descriptions allowing tens of thousands of hackers to run rampant across Hotmail." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlook.com#Security_issues))
- "Outlook.com has promised to respect users' privacy, specifically targeting Gmail's privacy practices." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlook.com#Security_and_privacy))
- "Outlook.com does not scan emails or attachments for advertising information and personal conversations are entirely ad-free." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlook.com#Security_and_privacy))
