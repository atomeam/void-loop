## Key milestones

- 2006: "Spotify was founded in April 2006 in Stockholm, Sweden, by Daniel Ek and Martin Lorentzon." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify#History))
- 2008: "Conceived as a legal alternative to music piracy, the service launched in several European markets in October 2008 and in the United States in July 2011." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify#History))
- 2018: "It became publicly traded on the New York Stock Exchange in April 2018 through a direct public offering." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify#History))
- 2024: "In 2024, it reported 252 million subscribers and 640 million monthly active users; by the end of 2025, these figures had risen to 290 million Premium subscribers and 751 million monthly active users." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify#History))

## Notable features

- "In Spotify's apps, music can be browsed or searched for via various parameters, such as artist, album, genre, playlist, or record label." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify#Features))
- "Users can create, edit and share playlists, share tracks on social media, and make playlists with other users." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify#Features))

## Controversies

- "Ahead of Spotify Wrapped 2024, Spotify changed its API so that modded versions of the client would not work." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify#Security))
- "Spotify has attracted significant criticism since its 2008 launch." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify#Reception))
- "The primary point of criticism centers around what artists, music creators, and the media have described as "unsustainable" compensation." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify#Reception))
- "Unlike physical sales or legal downloads (both of which were the main medium of listening to music at the time), which pay artists a fixed amount per song or album sold, Spotify pays royalties based on its "market share": the number of streams for their songs as a proportion of total songs streamed on the service." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify#Reception))
- "Spotify distributes approximately 70% of its total revenue to rights-holders, who will then pay artists based on their individual agreements." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify#Reception))
- "Spotify does not sell user data but did begin selling broader trend data, often coined "streaming intelligence" to marketing firms in 2016, allowing for the data to be available directly to clients." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify#Privacy_concerns))
