## Key milestones

- 2015: "In December 2015, Google added shared albums to Google Photos." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Photos#History))
- 2016: "In June 2016, Google updated Photos to include automatically generated albums." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Photos#History))
- 2017: "In February 2017, Google updated the "Albums" tab on the Android app to include three separate sections; one for the phone's camera roll, with different views for sorting options (such as people or location); another for photos taken inside other apps; and a third for the actual photo albums." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Photos#History))
- 2017: "In May 2017, Google announced several updates to Google Photos." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Photos#History))
- 2018: "In December 2018, Google doubled the number of photos and videos users can store in a private Google Photos Live Album." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Photos#History))
- 2019: "In September 2019, Google Photos introduced a new social media-like feature called "Memories" similar to the Stories feature in Instagram and Facebook which highlights past photos to give their users a nostalgic feeling." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Photos#History))
- 2024: "In March 2024, The New York Times reported that Google Photos was being used in a facial recognition program by Unit 8200, a surveillance unit of the Israeli Defense Forces, to surveil Palestinians in Gaza amid the Gaza war." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Photos#History))

## Ownership changes, pivots and shutdowns

- 2015: "It was announced in May 2015 and spun off from Google+, the company's former social network." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Photos))
- 2016: "On February 12, 2016, Google announced that the Picasa desktop application would be discontinued on March 15, 2016, followed by the closure of the Picasa Web Albums service on May 1, 2016." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Photos#History))

## Notable features

- "The service has apps for the Android and iOS operating systems, and a website." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Photos#Features))
- "Users back up their photos to the cloud service, which becomes accessible for all of their devices." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Photos#Features))
- "The Photos service analyzes and organizes images into groups and can identify features such as beaches, skylines, or "snowstorm in Toronto." From the application's search window, users are shown potential searches for groups of photos in three major categories: People, Places, and Things." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Photos#Features))

## Controversies

- "At the May 2015 release of Google Photos, reviewers wrote that the service was among the best of its kind." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Photos#Reception))
- "Walt Mossberg of Recode declared the service the best in cloud photo storage, against its competition from Amazon (Amazon Drive), Apple (iCloud), Dropbox, and Microsoft (OneDrive)." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Photos#Reception))
- "Jacob Kastrenakes of The Verge wrote that the release made Google a major competitor in the photo storage market, and that its pricing structure obsoleted the idea of paying for photo storage." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Photos#Reception))
- "Sarah Mitroff and Lynn La of CNET wrote that the service's phone and tablet apps were particularly good, and that Google Photos had a more streamlined design than Yahoo's Flickr and more organizing features than Apple's iCloud photo service." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Photos#Reception))
