## Key milestones

- 1988: "The predecessor of DW-TV was RIAS-TV, a television station launched by RIAS, a West Berlin broadcaster in August 1988." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DW-TV#History))
- 1990: "The fall of the Berlin Wall the following year and German reunification in 1990 led to the closure of RIAS-TV." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DW-TV#History))
- 1992: "On 1 April 1992, Deutsche Welle inherited RIAS-TV's broadcast facilities, using them to start a German and English-language television channel broadcast via satellite, DW (TV), adding a short Spanish broadcast segment the following year." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DW-TV#History))
- 1995: "In 1995, it began 24-hour operation (12 hours in German, 10 hours in English, two hours in Spanish)." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DW-TV#History))
- 2001: "In 2001, Deutsche Welle (in conjunction with ARD and ZDF) founded a subscription TV channel for North American viewers called German TV." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DW-TV#History))
- 2009: "In March 2009, DW-TV expanded its television services in Asia with two new channels: DW-TV Asia and DW-TV Asia+." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DW-TV#History))
- 2015: "Deutsche Welle changed its schedules again on 22 June 2015, with DW in Asia and Oceania and DW (Europe) merged to become a 24-hour English news channel." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DW-TV#History))
- 2020: "In the U.S., some of its programs were distributed via the World Channel as well as MHz Worldview, although after the closure of MHz Worldview in 2020, a few stations have since offered a full carriage of DW-TV." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DW-TV#History))

## Ownership changes, pivots and shutdowns

## Redesigns
