> ESPN is an American basic cable sports broadcasting network and the flagship property of ESPN, LLC, a joint venture of the Walt Disney Company, Hearst Communications (18%) and the National Football League (10%). Founded on September 7, 1979, by Bill Rasmussen, Scott Rasmussen, and Ed Eagan, it is widely considered as the biggest sports network, with its title being the "Worldwide Leader in Sports", with a 20% share interest of sports as a rise from previous years. ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN))

## Key milestones

- 1978: "Bill Rasmussen came up with the concept of ESPN in May 1978, after he was fired from his job with the World Hockey Association's New England Whalers." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN#Background_and_launch))
- 1979: "Available land to build their own facility was quickly found in Bristol, Connecticut (where the channel remains headquartered), with funding to buy the property provided by Getty Oil, which purchased 85% of the company from Bill Rasmussen on February 22, 1979, in an attempt to diversify the company's holdings." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN#Background_and_launch))
- 1979: "Another event that helped build ESPN's credibility was securing an advertising agreement with Anheuser-Busch in the spring of 1979; the company invested $1 million to be the "exclusive beer advertised on the network"." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN#Background_and_launch))
- 1979: "ESPN launched on September 7, 1979, beginning with the first telecast of what would become the channel's flagship program, SportsCenter." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN#Background_and_launch))
- 2026: "In April 2026, ESPN premiered a documentary, Sports Heaven: The Birth of ESPN, about the network's founding." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN#Background_and_launch))

## Ownership changes, pivots and shutdowns

- 1985: "Ownership of ABC, and thus control of ESPN, was acquired by Capital Cities Communications in 1985." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN#1980s_to_2000s))
- 1996: "Capital Cities/ABC Inc., was then acquired by the Walt Disney Company in 1996 and was re-branded as Walt Disney Television." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN#1980s_to_2000s))
- 2019: "On June 30, 2019, WatchESPN was discontinued with the service's full merger into the ESPN app." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN#Other_services))
- 2021: "It was discontinued on December 31, 2021." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN#Former_services))
- 2023: "On August 8, 2023, ESPN and Penn Entertainment announced a deal to rebrand Penn's sportsbooks with ESPN branding." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN#2020s))
- 2024: "It was discontinued on June 30, 2024, a day before the Longhorns' move to the Southeastern Conference." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN#Former_services))
- "ESPN's next big step came when the channel acquired the rights to broadcast coverage of the early rounds of the NCAA Division I men's basketball tournament." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN#1980s_to_2000s))
- "ESPN's parent company renamed themselves as Capital Cities/ABC Inc." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN#1980s_to_2000s))

## Notable features

- "ESPN HD ESPN launched its high definition simulcast feed, originally branded as ESPNHD, on March 30, 2003, with an Opening Day broadcast of the Texas Rangers and Anaheim Angels." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN#Other_services))
- "All current ESPN content is broadcast in high definition." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN#Other_services))
- "Pardon the Interruption and Around the Horn began airing in HD on September 27, 2010, with the relocation of the production of both shows into the facility housing the Washington, D.C., bureau for ABC News." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN#Other_services))
- "ESPN Classic ESPN Classic was a subscription television network that launched in 1995 as Classic Sports Network, founded by Brian Bedol and Steve Greenberg." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN#Former_services))
- "ESPN Inc., purchased Classic Sports Network in 1997 for $175 million, rebranding the channel as "ESPN Classic" in 1998." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN#Former_services))
- "The channel broadcast notable archived sporting events (originally including events from earlier decades, but later focusing mainly on events from the 1990s and later), as well as sports documentaries and sports-themed movies." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN#Former_services))

## Controversies

- "ESPN has been criticized for focusing too much on men's college and professional sports (particularly the NBA and NFL), and very little on women's sports or extreme sports." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN#Criticism))
- "Baseball, ice hockey, and soccer fans have also criticized ESPN for not giving their respective sports more coverage." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN#Criticism))
- "Other criticism has focused on ethnicity in ESPN's varying mediated forms, as well as carriage fees and issues regarding the exportation of ESPN content." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN#Criticism))
- "Some critics argue that ESPN's success is their ability to provide other enterprise and investigative sports news while competing with other hard sports-news-producing outlets such as Yahoo!" ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN#Criticism))
