## Ownership changes, pivots and shutdowns

- 1996: "By the time the network was launched on 1 November 1996, many former workers of the first BBC Arabic television station, which had closed down in April of that year following censorship attempts by the Saudi government, were hired by Al Jazeera." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_Arabic#Launch_and_initial_coverage_(1996%E2%80%931999)))
- 1996: "The channel began broadcasting in late 1996, with many staff joining from the BBC World Service's Saudi-co-owned Arabic-language TV station, which had shut down on 1 April 1996 after two years of operation because of censorship demands by the Saudi Arabian government." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_Arabic#Organization))
- 2013: "It had been available through live streaming over the Al Jazeera website, DVB-S, Galaxy 19, free to air and Galaxy 23 satellites, and it had been broadcast over the air in the Washington, DC DMA by WNVC on digital channel 30–5, and on digital channel 48.2 in the New York metro area, but those broadcasts were discontinued on 20 August 2013." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_Arabic#Availability))
- 2014: "Al Jazeera launched a Turkish-language news website in 2014; it was shut down on 3 May 2017." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_Arabic#Expansion))
- 2017: "On 23 June 2017, the countries that cut ties to Qatar issued a list of demands to end the crisis, insisting that Qatar shut down the Al Jazeera network, close a Turkish military base and scale down ties with Iran." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_Arabic#Qatar_diplomatic_crisis_(2017%E2%80%932021)))
- 2019: "On 20 September 2019, Twitter announced it had shut down two groups of accounts with links to UAE spreading disinformation primarily aimed against Qatar." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_Arabic#United_Arab_Emirates_lobbying_(2019)))
- "The call, included in a list of 13 points, read: "Shut down Al Jazeera and its affiliate stations." Agencies, media outlets, journalists and media rights organizations decried the demands to close Al Jazeera as attempts to curb press freedom, including Reporters Without Borders; The Guardian, The New York Times, and the Committee to Protect Journalists (CPJ)." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_Arabic#Qatar_diplomatic_crisis_(2017%E2%80%932021)))
- "The acquisition of Current TV by Al Jazeera allowed Time Warner Cable to drop the network due to its low ratings, but they released a statement saying that they would consider carrying the channel after they evaluated whether it made sense for their customers." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_Arabic#Al_Jazeera_America))

## Controversies

- "While Al Jazeera has a large audience in the Middle East, the organization and the original Arabic channel in particular have been criticised and involved in a number of controversies." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_Arabic#Controversies_and_criticism))
- "Although Al Jazeera has denied that it its editorial process is interfered with by its state owners, Al Jazeera has been accused of supporting the stances of the Qatari government on regional issues, with the BBC describing its Arabic-language coverage as more biased than that of Al Jazeera English, and giving favourable coverage of Muslim Brotherhood aligned Islamist groups." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_Arabic#Controversies_and_criticism))
- "At the onset of the Qatar diplomatic crisis in 2017, the governments of Saudi Arabia, Egypt, the United Arab Emirates and Bahrain called for the closure of the entire Al Jazeera conglomerate as part of a list of thirteen demands that were presented to the Qatari government in exchange for re-normalized relations, though this never came to pass." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_Arabic#Controversies_and_criticism))
