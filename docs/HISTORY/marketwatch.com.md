## Key milestones

- 1995: "The company was conceived as DBC Online by Data Broadcasting Corporation in the fall of 1995." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MarketWatch#History))
- 1997: "The marketwatch.com domain name was registered on July 30, 1997." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MarketWatch#History))
- 1997: "The website launched on October 30, 1997, as a 50/50 joint venture between DBC and CBS News, then run by Larry Kramer and co-founder and chairman, Derek Reisfield." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MarketWatch#History))
- 1999: "In 1999, the company hired David Callaway and in 2003, Callaway became editor-in-chief." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MarketWatch#History))
- 1999: "In January 1999, during the dot-com bubble, the company became a public company via an initial public offering." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MarketWatch#History))
- 2000: "In June 2000, the company formed a joint venture with the Financial Times with Peter Bale as managing editor." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MarketWatch#History))
- 2004: "In January 2004, Calandra resigned amidst allegations of insider trading." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MarketWatch#History))
- 2016: "In May 2016, MarketWatch hired Dan Shar as general manager." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MarketWatch#History))
- 2020: "In October 2020, MarketWatch announced that it would become a paywalled subscription-based publication, in order to "raise the ambitions of our journalism"." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MarketWatch#History))
- 2022: "Mark DeCambre was named editor in chief on March 21, 2022." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MarketWatch#History))

## Ownership changes, pivots and shutdowns

- 2005: "In January 2005, Dow Jones & Company acquired the company for $528 million, or $18 per share." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MarketWatch#History))
