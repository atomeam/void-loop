## Key milestones

- 1964: "In 1964 it moved to London, losing some of its regional agenda but continuing to be heavily subsidised by sales of the more downmarket but more profitable Manchester Evening News." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian#Publication_history))
- 1993: "In 1993 the paper declined to participate in the broadsheet price war started by Rupert Murdoch's The Times." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian#Publication_history))
- 1993: "In June 1993, The Guardian bought The Observer from Lonrho, thus gaining a serious Sunday sister newspaper with similar political views." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian#Publication_history))
- 2007: "The Guardian Weekly was also linked to a website for expatriates, Guardian Abroad, which was launched in 2007 but had been taken offline by 2012." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian#Publication_history))
- 2023: "They live on strife ... ." In March 2023, an academic review commissioned by the Scott Trust determined that John Edward Taylor and nine of his eleven backers had links to the Atlantic slave trade through their interests in Manchester's textile industry." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian#Early_years))

## Ownership changes, pivots and shutdowns

- 1993: "The Guardian Media Group acquired the Sunday newspaper The Observer in June 1993, after a rival acquisition bid by The Independent was rejected." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian#The_Observer))
- 2020: "On 1 July 2020, Guardian Soulmates was closed down with the explanation: "The online dating world is a very different place to when we first launched online in July 2004." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian#Online_media))
- 2025: "The Observer was sold to Tortoise Media, effective from April 2025." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian#The_Observer))
- "They do not toil, neither do they spin, but they live better than those that do." When the government closed down the Manchester Observer, the mill-owners' champions had the upper hand." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian#Early_years))
- "In 1825, the paper merged with the British Volunteer and was known as The Manchester Guardian and British Volunteer until 1828." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian#Early_years))

## Redesigns

- 1988: "On 12 February 1988, The Guardian had a significant redesign; as well as improving the quality of its printers' ink, it also changed its masthead to a juxtaposition of an italic Garamond "The", with a bold Helvetica "Guardian", that remained in use until the 2005 redesign." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian#Publication_history))
- 1988: "Designed by Mark Porter, the new look includes a new masthead for the newspaper, its first since 1988." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian#Moving_to_the_Berliner_paper_format))
- 1992: "In 1992, The Guardian relaunched its features section as G2, a tabloid-format supplement." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian#Publication_history))
- "The format switch was accompanied by a comprehensive redesign of the paper's look." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian#Moving_to_the_Berliner_paper_format))
- "A typeface family designed by Paul Barnes and Christian Schwartz was created for the new design." ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian#Moving_to_the_Berliner_paper_format))
