## What existing implementations do

Sensory substitution maps one channel onto another. The product habit is a camera, an array, and a restoration sentence.

- Bach-y-Rita and colleagues (1969, Nature) projected a camera image onto the back as a tactile pattern. Later arrays moved to the tongue or fingertip. That is a lab apparatus, not a local note.
- Meijer (1992) encoded a visual frame as sound: brightness to loudness, horizontal position to time, vertical position to pitch. The vOICe still ships that idea. A 2025 white paper by Meijer compares implants for restoring vision with non-invasive visual-to-auditory substitution and treats the two as different claims, not as one seal.
- BrainPort Vision Pro, from the maker's public page, is an oral electronic aid: a wearable camera, a tongue array, white pixels as stronger stimulation. The maker says some users describe it as seeing with the tongue, and also that a user may feel a disk rather than see a sighted image. That split is the point. A description is not a restored sense.
- A 2026 hippocampal subfield paper, pointed at on 2026-10-03 by @seeingwithsound (`2106345229401928146`) and glossed by @purecity_life (`2106359974243008832`), used a tactile sensory substitution device in a real-world obstacle course. Those posts are outside this run's strict 24-hour window. They are design material, not a score.
- Run 055 already drafted a three-glyph tone map with a Play click and Web Audio. This run does not repeat that. No tone is generated. No glyph is played.
