## What existing implementations do

Sensory substitution packs one channel into another and often talks as if the packing is the missing sense.

- Bach-y-Rita and colleagues (1969) put a camera image on the back as touch. Later tongue and fingertip arrays kept the camera-plus-array habit. That is lab apparatus, not a local note.
- Meijer (1992) mapped a frame to sound: brightness to loudness, horizontal position to time, vertical position to pitch. The vOICe still ships that sequential scan. A 2025 review (Butorova and Sergeev, European Physical Journal Special Topics) sorts visual-to-auditory methods into column-by-column sonification, one complex signal for the whole image, and depth-led 3D sonification, and notes sensory-cognitive overload. Those are encoding classes.
- BrainPort-class tongue arrays and Colorophone-class phone maps still pair a camera with a substitute channel. A maker page can say both “seeing with the tongue” and “felt a disk.” The split is the point. A description is not a restored sense.
- On 2026-10-04, @seeingwithsound (`2106666313414582450`) quoted a Hilbert-curve depth sonification paper: unlike sequential scanning, the system “represents entire scenes simultaneously.” That post is outside this run’s strict 24-hour window. It is design material, not a score. In-window, the same account posted a crossmodal-plasticity viewpoint (`2107143294740320618`) and an ICMS selectivity link (`2107485259688472931`). A plasticity paper is not a scene.
