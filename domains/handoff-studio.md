Owner-extraction studio that keeps profitable Main Street firms from dying when the owner retires: capture the business stuck in their heads → living operating twin → transferability / exit-readiness pack → quiet matched SBA buyer (or employee-ownership path) → 90-day handoff + company-only AI co-pilot that stays with the firm. ## The problem (national stats — validate before external use)

By ~2035, about **6 million** U.S. SMBs will change hands or shut down as owners age. McKinsey-adjacent estimates: **>1 million** viable sale candidates, up to **~$5T** enterprise value.

What happens today:

- In a recent year, ~**510k** SMB exits: ~**92% closed**, only ~**5% sold**, ~**3%** transferred (often family). Try-to-sell success often cited only **~20–30%**.
- Roughly **half** of owners 55+ have no real succession plan; only ~**32%** have a documented exit plan; ~**80%** of businesses that list never sell.
- ~**80%** of projected exits sit under **~$2M** — too small for PE, messy for first-time buyers. Search funds / PE want cleaner books and higher EBITDA; first-time buyers need SBA-ready financials, a transition plan, and a seller **90-day stay**.
- A 2026 Zelle survey: **41%** of owners 50+ would shut down rather than keep operating if they cannot find a qualified buyer.
- Towns quietly lose HVAC, landscaping, dental labs, machine shops — the fabric of local service.

McKinsey-adjacent claim (validate before external use): a working transfer market could protect up to ~**12M jobs** and ~**$250B/yr** local spending.

This is not a "silver tsunami of cheap listings." Listed deals are competitive. The neglected inventory is **off-market**: owners who have not cleaned books, have no successor, and will quietly lock the door — jobs, local tax base, and retirement wealth disappear.

Second topical pressure: the same trades (HVAC, electrical, plumbing, fabrication) already run ~**three openings per trained worker**; data-center buildout pulls more away. Closing those shops worsens the shortage.

**Why this is good:** the job is not inventing a market — it is unblocking the **translation layer** between retirement capital stuck in Main Street shops and SBA / operator ambition that cannot underwrite messy books.

## Product: AfterOwner (engine inside Handoff Studio)

AfterOwner is the operational engine that makes Handoff Studio work. Selling "I prep businesses for SBA buyers" sounds like a broker. Selling "I build an operating twin of the owner's brain so the company doesn't die when they retire" is the product.

**Weld (sale-ready ↔ AfterOwner):** the **sale-ready package** is the financeability layer (SDE recast, lender-shaped folder, SBA 7(a) file); the **AfterOwner twin** is the ops-continuity / transferability atom that strengthens that package — one studio, two layers, neither erases the other.

| Layer | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Owner-extraction studio** | 4–8 week shadow / exit-readiness of the retiring owner — judgment calls, vendor relationships, exception handling, tacit "how we actually do it" |
| **Living operating twin** | Structured capture of those calls into a usable twin the buyer (and later the co-pilot) can run against |
| **Transferability / exit-readiness pack** | SBA-ready package: clean books to tax-return quality, SDE story, 12 operating procedures, vendor map, key-person risk burn-down, 90-day stay-on plan, one-page CIM — docs a first-time buyer and lender can underwrite |
| **Matched owner (quiet)** | Confidential match from three pools: (a) SBA-ready owner-operators in the trade, (b) GM / lead tech with SBA + seller note, (c) worker-ownership when no outside buyer fits — no public blast listings |
| **Handoff (90 days post-close)** | Built into purchase agreement: seller consulting schedule, top-20 account intros, estimator/dispatcher trained, scoped non-compete — the part brokers skip |
| **Company-only AI co-pilot** | Co-pilot trained exclusively on *this* firm's extracted twin; stays with the company post-close (recurring SaaS tail; not a generic chatbot) |

## Capital stack & pricing (SBA fluency)

For complete change of ownership under ~$5M, **SBA 7(a)** is the workhorse:

- **10% equity injection** of total project cost (stays under Oct 2026 SOP).
- At least **half of that 10%** = buyer's own cash; other half often a **seller note on full standby**.
- **$1M example:** ~$50k buyer cash + ~$50k seller standby + ~$900k SBA loan. Zero-down deals are gone.
- **Goodwill** is financable under 7(a); non-RE maturity typically **~10 years**.
- Package already **lender-shaped** = the edge (messy books die in underwriting).

### Timing — late 2026 / SOP 50 10 8.1 (Oct 1, 2026)

Ownership wave is now **and** lending tightens. SOP **50 10 8.1** (effective **Oct 1, 2026** — verify before citing externally):

- DSCR floor on many **initial acquisitions**: historical cash-flow coverage **1.15x → 1.25x**.
- **QoE mandatory at $3M+**.
- **10%** equity injection stays.
- Messy books die in underwriting. **Packaging to the new lending standard is the bottleneck** — not inventing demand.

**FY2025 volume claims (verify before citing externally):** ~$8.29B across ~7,003 7(a) acquisition deals; median ticket ~$1.18M; lower default rate than non-acquisition 7(a) — treat as claims to verify, not Face copy.

**Main Street pricing:** typically **2.5x–4x SDE** (not tech multiples). A shop at ~$400k SDE often lands ~$1.0M–$1.6M if the owner is not the only rainmaker.

**Wedge:** readiness + off-market sourcing + SBA packaging. Only take a listing **after** the package. Already know which buyers can get a loan; already know whether the manager can buy it. Teamshares / search funds / roll-ups eat the larger end — the gap is the $400k–$2M shop with 8–25 people.
