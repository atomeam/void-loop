## What is now known

- **Coral restoration moves reef-accretion potential (primary ocean result).** Toth, Johnson, Lyons, Spadaro, Stathakopoulos, Bloomer, Mallon, Jenkins, Williams, Combs, Craig, and Muller, "Coral restoration can drive rapid increases in reef-accretion potential," *Scientific Reports*, DOI [10.1038/s41598-025-04818-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-04818-3) (USGS pubs record [70270065](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70270065); PubMed [40759666](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40759666/)). Paired restored vs non-restored surveys at eight offshore and three inshore Lower Florida Keys reefs (Mote Marine Laboratory outplants; USGS surveys summers 2022–2023). Within **2–6 years** after outplanting, restoring fast-growing *Acropora cervicornis* raised average reef-accretion potential to **2.80 mm y⁻¹ (±0.81)** on offshore restored areas vs **−0.84 mm y⁻¹ (±0.32)** on paired non-restored areas (paired t-test t₉ = −5.79, p = 0.001). Gross carbonate production rose **>16-fold** (about **3.62 vs 0.22 kg CaCO₃ m⁻² y⁻¹**) with about **5% (±0.68 SE)** average cover gain and significant gains in structural complexity. Inshore massive-coral restoration showed **no measurable** accretion-potential effect (cover ≈9% restored and unrestored). The severe **2023 bleaching** event right after the study caused near-complete *A. cervicornis* mortality while **59% of massive corals survived**, so growth-vs-survival trade-offs stay open for ecosystem-scale persistence. Authors conclude restoration can produce rapid **small-scale** accretion gains while ecosystem-scale persistence remains uncertain.
- **Open USGS datasets for the same sites (CC0).** Carbonate budgets, Structure-from-Motion products, and topographic complexity: DOI [10.5066/P13HMEON](https://www.usgs.gov/data/carbonate-budgets-structure-motion-products-and-topographic-complexity-measurements-restored) (published 2025-06-24). Companion imagery: Johnson et al. 2025, DOI [10.5066/P1WHKTRD](https://doi.org/10.5066/P1WHKTRD). These are the machine-readable path for a small team or AI without running new field campaigns.
- **Global coral restoration methods baseline.** ICRI hosts the Coral Restoration Database built from Boström-Einarsson et al. (review / Dryad), synthesizing peer-reviewed, grey literature, and practitioner surveys into an open case-study corpus ([ICRI page](https://icriforum.org/restoration/coral-restoration-database/), accessed 2026-10-03). Useful for method cataloguing; the Toth carbonate-budget numbers are the sharper geo-ecological metric.
- **Decadal temperate octocoral functional recovery (open Dryad).** Active restoration of *Corallium rubrum* drove functional recovery over ten years; data and code on Dryad DOI [10.5061/dryad.j3tx95xrn](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.j3tx95xrn) (published 2025-02-14). Complements tropical branching-coral accretion with a temperate trait-recovery example.
- **Soil organic carbon after land-use change (open global ledger).** Shuai et al., GSOCS-LULCC: *Scientific Data* [s41597-026-07749-4](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-026-07749-4); Zenodo DOI [10.5281/zenodo.20774640](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20774640) (v2.1 updated through papers to Dec 2025; Zenodo record also listed at [20500307](https://zenodo.org/records/20500307)). **8,748** harmonized SOC-stock records from **1,967** sites across five land uses, compiled from **912** peer-reviewed articles. Direct path for AI-readable soil / land-cover contribution maps.
- **National soil resample with management history.** Australia SOC-M / Scientific Data key-attributes paper [s41597-026-07850-8](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-026-07850-8): **308** agricultural sites baseline 2009–2012 and resampled 2022–2025, with SOC fractions and paddock management 2010–2023 aligned to ACCU measurement methods ([ANDS / researchdata.edu.au entry](https://researchdata.edu.au/soil-organic-carbon-monitoring-project/4013354), available 2026-02-05).
- **Reforestation co-deployment signal (carbon, not yet a Void card).** Large Wales trial (11.5 ha, 6,400 trees, four years): enhanced rock weathering via crushed silicate rock raised aboveground carbon stocks **27%** in broadleaf stands by 2024 (+**787 kg C ha⁻¹**); soil-microbiome inoculation effects varied by species and faded (*Communications Sustainability* [s44458-026-00103-0](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44458-026-00103-0)). Logged as neighbour context; Florida Keys ocean card stays first for Void because it has both a clear public action story and CC0 survey data.

### Heat survival after the 2023 marine heatwave (added 2026-10-07 18:07 ET)

- **The 2023 heatwave erased the accretion gains: functional extinction of Florida's Acropora.** Manzello, Cunning, Karp, Baker and 44 co-authors, "Heat-driven functional extinction of Caribbean *Acropora* corals from Florida's Coral Reef," *Science* 390(6771):361-366, published 23 Oct 2025, DOI [10.1126/science.adx7825](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adx7825) ([USGS record 70273765](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70273765); [PubMed 41129628](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41129628/)). Sea surface temperatures stayed at or above 31 °C for an average of 40.7 days, giving heat exposure 2.2 to 4 times higher than any prior year on record, with a 32.3 °C peak in July 2023 ([AAAS release on EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1102462), 23 Oct 2025). Of 52,356 wild and restored colonies tracked, 97.8 to 100% of *A. palmata* and *A. cervicornis* in the Florida Keys and Dry Tortugas were dead by March 2024; mortality offshore southeast Florida was 37.9% because the water there was cooler. This settles the caveat the Toth et al. result carried: the restored offshore staghorn stands that drove the +2.80 mm y⁻¹ accretion flip were inside the zone that lost nearly everything.
- **Mission: Iconic Reefs field check agreed early.** NOAA, Mote and Coral Restoration Foundation surveyed 64 locations at five of the seven Mission: Iconic Reefs sites after the event: preliminary data showed under 22% of about 1,500 outplanted staghorn alive, living staghorn only at Carysfort and Horseshoe (the two northernmost), and no live staghorn or elkhorn in the Looe Key sample areas ([NOAA news release](https://www.noaa.gov/news-release/researchers-assess-florida-keys-coral-health-following-marine-heat-wave), accessed 2026-10-07). The Science census later found the full toll was higher.
- **Restoration infrastructure is what kept the species alive.** Muller, Ladd, Karp, Montoya-Maya, Kuffner, Baker, Miller and colleagues, "Success of restoration strategies in preventing extirpation of 2 critically endangered coral species," *Conservation Biology* 40(2), 2025, DOI [10.1111/cobi.70168](https://doi.org/10.1111/cobi.70168) ([NOAA repository, CC BY-NC-ND](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/73739)). Two independent land-based living gene banks, redundant ocean and land nurseries, emergency evacuation of nursery corals, and early shading and cooling trials prevented regional extirpation and kept much of the genetic richness. Of 158 elkhorn genets in the upper Keys before 2023, 23 remain in the wild and the rest live only under human care (Baker et al., Science policy forum, below).
- **Symbiont type is the strongest single heat-tolerance lever measured in Florida elkhorn.** "Heat-tolerant algal symbionts may prevent extirpation of the threatened elkhorn coral, *Acropora palmata*, in Florida during intensifying marine heatwaves," *Coral Reefs*, published 22 Apr 2025, DOI [10.1007/s00338-025-02652-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00338-025-02652-7) ([NSF PAR 10610530](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10610530)). CBASS acute heat assays in June 2022 on 172 colonies (125 genets, four nurseries) found a 4.17 °C spread in ED50; nursery explained 17.2%, genet 25.9%, symbiont abundance 15.6%. The 10 colonies hosting *Durusdinium* averaged 1.9 °C more heat tolerant than those hosting *Symbiodinium*, which the authors estimate would have cut the effective 2023 heat dose by about 92%. Their recommendation: rear sexually produced recruits that host *Durusdinium*, and bring in elkhorn diversity from outside Florida.
- **Staghorn tolerance varies within nurseries.** Cunning et al. censused 229 staghorn colonies across six Florida nurseries with CBASS and found about a 2.5 °C spread in ED50 with reproducible rankings (r = 0.76) ([NSF PAR 10311438](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10311438), Proc. R. Soc. B 2021). A 2025 Little Cayman nursery record shows why that matters: at 19.4 degree heating weeks, 91.6% of 415 staghorn colonies died and 7 of 10 genotypes were wiped out (*Frontiers in Marine Science*, DOI [10.3389/fmars.2025.1599155](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmars.2025.1599155), 2025).
- **The CBASS rig is cheap, open, and maker-buildable.** The published method uses eight 16 L party coolers, aquarium heaters and chillers on custom Arduino controllers, and 165 W LED grow lights, ramping fragments to 32 to 38 °C over 3 h, holding 3 h, then reading photosynthetic efficiency to fit an ED50 ([NSF PAR 10562973](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10562973), method retest of 67 staghorn genets). This is the assay a small team can copy.
- **Assisted gene flow moved from proposal to the reef.** Baker et al., "Proactive assisted gene flow for Caribbean corals in an era of rapid coral reef decline," *Science* 389(6758):344-347, 24 Jul 2025, DOI [10.1126/science.adx5842](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adx5842) ([PubMed 40705881](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40705881/)), argues for regulated cross-border breeding of Caribbean corals raised in closed systems. The Coral Restoration Consortium genetics group white paper (Baums et al. 2025, [AWI ePIC](https://epic.awi.de/id/eprint/60026/)) recommends lab-bred juveniles over wild adults, scaled to Florida's founder population.
- **First permitted cross-border coral outplant, July 2025.** University of Miami Rosenstiel School, Tela Marine (Honduras) and The Florida Aquarium bred Florida elkhorn with parents from warm, turbid Tela Bay, Honduras (more than 200 offspring), and outplanted the first 35 Flonduran plus 35 Florida-only juveniles off Miami with Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission support ([UM news, 15 Jul 2025](https://news.miami.edu/rosenstiel/stories/2025/07/international-collaboration-provides-hope-for-the-future-of-florida-and-caribbean-coral-reefs.html)). An earlier Florida x Curaçao cohort was not approved for outplant and stays in an aquarium; the Honduran population is genetically closer to Florida ([Miami Herald](https://www.miamiherald.com/news/local/environment/climate-change/article310132930.html), Jul 2025). In a land nursery, Curaçao x Florida crosses carried 879 alleles not found in Florida genets, and the Florida x Florida cohort was the most heat-sensitive (bioRxiv [10.1101/2025.03.03.641242](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.03.03.641242), preprint, 3 Mar 2025).
- **Early field result: the Flondurans came through summer 2025.** Tela Marine reported on 15 Jan 2026 that the Key Biscayne Flondurans it follows (it gives 25 planted) all came through the summer heat and are growing in diameter and upward ([Tela Coral](https://telacoral.org/flondurans-still-flourishing/), 15 Jan 2026). The count differs from UM's 35, and no side-by-side survival numbers against the Florida-only controls are published yet. Summer 2025 was a real stress year in the Keys: most of the sanctuary reached NOAA Bleaching Alert Level 2 with all stations above 30 °C on 27 Aug 2025, and temperatures stayed above historical maximums until early October ([Mote BleachWatch 27 Aug 2025](https://mote.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/CC_20250827.pdf); [22 Oct 2025](https://mote.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/CC_20251022.pdf)). Key Biscayne sits north of the sanctuary, so its own heat dose still needs checking.
- **Dry Tortugas trial, April 2026.** UM, USGS St. Petersburg and the National Park Service outplanted nearly three dozen two-year-old Flonduran and same-age Florida elkhorn side by side at three Dry Tortugas National Park sites, with checks every six months for growth, survival and health ([UM news, 19 May 2026](https://news.miami.edu/rosenstiel/stories/2026/05/scientists-deploy-experimental-elkhorn-corals-to-dry-tortugas-to-test-introduction-of-new-genetic-diversity.html)). The first six-month check falls around October 2026, after this summer's heat.

## What changed this cycle

2026-10-07 18:07 ET: open question 3 has its answer, and it corrects the framing of the Florida Keys card. The Toth et al. accretion flip is real and measured, and it was erased within a year by a heatwave that killed nearly every Acropora in the Keys. The planet-restoration lesson is now sharper: planting fast-growing corals builds reef quickly, and heat tolerance decides whether that reef lasts. The track's frontier moved from "does restoration add reef?" to "can restoration pick or breed corals that live through the next heatwave?", and that question has a live, dated field test (Flonduran vs Florida-only, Key Biscayne since July 2025 and Dry Tortugas since April 2026).

2026-10-03 12:11 ET: planet restoration left the stub state with a dated ocean result, open USGS files, and two open soil ledgers.

## Concrete experiment

Two parts, both doable by a small team or an AI with no field boat.

1. Heat-dose tracker: pull NOAA Coral Reef Watch 5 km degree heating weeks for Key Biscayne, Dry Tortugas, Carysfort and Looe Key from summer 2023 to now, and line each site's heat dose up against every published survival checkpoint (Manzello 2025, NOAA Mission: Iconic Reefs survey, Tela 15 Jan 2026, the coming Dry Tortugas checks). Output: one table showing which heat doses killed which corals, so a 2026 survival report can be read against the heat it actually faced.
2. Bench CBASS: build the published cooler rig (eight coolers, Arduino temperature control, heaters, chillers, LED lights) and validate it on a heat-tough aquarium coral such as *Montipora* or *Pocillopora* fragments from a licensed aquarium supplier, reproducing a stable ED50 ranking across two runs (target r above 0.7, as Cunning et al. got). The controller enclosure and fragment racks are printable parts, which ties this track to the printing track.
