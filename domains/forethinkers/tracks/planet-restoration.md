## What is now known

- **Coral restoration moves reef-accretion potential (primary ocean result).** Toth, Johnson, Lyons, Spadaro, Stathakopoulos, Bloomer, Mallon, Jenkins, Williams, Combs, Craig, and Muller, "Coral restoration can drive rapid increases in reef-accretion potential," *Scientific Reports*, DOI [10.1038/s41598-025-04818-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-04818-3) (USGS pubs record [70270065](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70270065); PubMed [40759666](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40759666/)). Paired restored vs non-restored surveys at eight offshore and three inshore Lower Florida Keys reefs (Mote Marine Laboratory outplants; USGS surveys summers 2022–2023). Within **2–6 years** after outplanting, restoring fast-growing *Acropora cervicornis* raised average reef-accretion potential to **2.80 mm y⁻¹ (±0.81)** on offshore restored areas vs **−0.84 mm y⁻¹ (±0.32)** on paired non-restored areas (paired t-test t₉ = −5.79, p = 0.001). Gross carbonate production rose **>16-fold** (about **3.62 vs 0.22 kg CaCO₃ m⁻² y⁻¹**) with about **5% (±0.68 SE)** average cover gain and significant gains in structural complexity. Inshore massive-coral restoration showed **no measurable** accretion-potential effect (cover ≈9% restored and unrestored). The severe **2023 bleaching** event right after the study caused near-complete *A. cervicornis* mortality while **59% of massive corals survived**, so growth-vs-survival trade-offs stay open for ecosystem-scale persistence. Authors conclude restoration can produce rapid **small-scale** accretion gains while ecosystem-scale persistence remains uncertain.
- **Open USGS datasets for the same sites (CC0).** Carbonate budgets, Structure-from-Motion products, and topographic complexity: DOI [10.5066/P13HMEON](https://www.usgs.gov/data/carbonate-budgets-structure-motion-products-and-topographic-complexity-measurements-restored) (published 2025-06-24). Companion imagery: Johnson et al. 2025, DOI [10.5066/P1WHKTRD](https://doi.org/10.5066/P1WHKTRD). These are the machine-readable path for a small team or AI without running new field campaigns.
- **Global coral restoration methods baseline.** ICRI hosts the Coral Restoration Database built from Boström-Einarsson et al. (review / Dryad), synthesizing peer-reviewed, grey literature, and practitioner surveys into an open case-study corpus ([ICRI page](https://icriforum.org/restoration/coral-restoration-database/), accessed 2026-10-03). Useful for method cataloguing; the Toth carbonate-budget numbers are the sharper geo-ecological metric.
- **Decadal temperate octocoral functional recovery (open Dryad).** Active restoration of *Corallium rubrum* drove functional recovery over ten years; data and code on Dryad DOI [10.5061/dryad.j3tx95xrn](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.j3tx95xrn) (published 2025-02-14). Complements tropical branching-coral accretion with a temperate trait-recovery example.
- **Soil organic carbon after land-use change (open global ledger).** Shuai et al., GSOCS-LULCC: *Scientific Data* [s41597-026-07749-4](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-026-07749-4); Zenodo DOI [10.5281/zenodo.20774640](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20774640) (v2.1 updated through papers to Dec 2025; Zenodo record also listed at [20500307](https://zenodo.org/records/20500307)). **8,748** harmonized SOC-stock records from **1,967** sites across five land uses, compiled from **912** peer-reviewed articles. Direct path for AI-readable soil / land-cover contribution maps.
- **National soil resample with management history.** Australia SOC-M / Scientific Data key-attributes paper [s41597-026-07850-8](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-026-07850-8): **308** agricultural sites baseline 2009–2012 and resampled 2022–2025, with SOC fractions and paddock management 2010–2023 aligned to ACCU measurement methods ([ANDS / researchdata.edu.au entry](https://researchdata.edu.au/soil-organic-carbon-monitoring-project/4013354), available 2026-02-05).
- **Reforestation co-deployment signal (carbon, not yet a Void card).** Large Wales trial (11.5 ha, 6,400 trees, four years): enhanced rock weathering via crushed silicate rock raised aboveground carbon stocks **27%** in broadleaf stands by 2024 (+**787 kg C ha⁻¹**); soil-microbiome inoculation effects varied by species and faded (*Communications Sustainability* [s44458-026-00103-0](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44458-026-00103-0)). Logged as neighbour context; Florida Keys ocean card stays first for Void because it has both a clear public action story and CC0 survey data.
