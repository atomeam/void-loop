## What is now known

- **A whole offline voice mind fits a toy-sized board today.** M5Stack Module LLM ([product docs](https://docs.m5stack.com/en/module/Module-LLM), accessed 2026-10-07): Axera AX630C SoC (dual Cortex-A53 1.2 GHz, NPU 3.2 TOPS INT8), 4 GB LPDDR4, 32 GB eMMC, on-board mic and 1 W speaker, built-in wake word, speech recognition, Qwen2.5-0.5B language model and text-to-speech running with no cloud. 54.0 x 54.0 x 13.0 mm, 17.1 g. Power 0.5 W idle, 1.5 W full load at 5 V. Models need Axera's own format, so the model list is set by the vendor's converted builds. The standalone module is marked end-of-life in the [M5Stack shop](https://shop.m5stack.com/products/m5stack-llm-large-laguage-model-module-ax630c) (listed $49.90, accessed 2026-10-07), so a product design names the AX630C chip family and the LLM630 compute kit as the path, not this one SKU.
- **It answers fast enough for play.** On the same module, Qwen2.5-0.5B (128-token context) gives a 359.8 ms first token and 10.32 tokens/s; the INT4 build gives 442.95 ms and 12.52 tokens/s; the 256-token context build takes 1,126 ms to first token ([M5Stack model page](https://docs.m5stack.com/en/stackflow/models/qwen2.5-0.5b-instruct), accessed 2026-10-07). Derived here: a 30-token reply (about 20 words) is ready in roughly 3.3 s of generation, and streaming speech can start after the first few tokens. Compare the open CantaStorie build on an Arduino UNO Q with Gemma 3 1B through llama.cpp, which took about 27 s before audio started after optimisation ([Hackster.io, Nick Bild](https://www.hackster.io/news/an-ai-toy-that-doesn-t-need-the-cloud-4673762c2f8c), posted about 2026-10-03, accessed 2026-10-07). The NPU path is roughly an order of magnitude quicker, which is the difference between a toy that converses and one that narrates.
- **Power for a session is a sizing job, not a research gap.** Derived here from the 1.5 W full-load figure: a 2,000 mAh 3.7 V cell holds 7.4 Wh, so at about 85% conversion efficiency it runs the mind at full load for about 4 hours and idle for about 12. Servos and speaker come on top and set the real budget, which is why open question 1 asks for a measured session.
- **The size points to a "cupboard" layout.** The 54 mm board is larger than Motelet's 38 mm body, so the mind fits the figure's home base, stand or carry case, with mic, speaker and servos in the figure on a thin cable or short radio link. This also matches the book: the cupboard brings the figure to life. Status: design hypothesis, recorded so the bench test in open question 1 checks it.
- **Consumer toys with on-device minds are arriving in 2026 (corrected 2026-10-15, see the Babaru correction below).** Babaru (PMS International): a talking plush described as the first small-LLM offline AI toy, memory stored locally, no data sharing, no subscription, free companion app, ten languages, retail launch Q3 2026, aimed at the kidult market ([Toy World Magazine](https://toyworldmag.co.uk/pms-international-brings-babaru-to-market/), 16 Apr 2026). Waylo: a plush bear for ages 3 to 8 sold in an "Offline" edition at a €199 pre-order price (list €349) with no Wi-Fi or subscription and an online edition at €249 ([waylo.ai](https://waylo.ai/), accessed 2026-10-07). Both are company claims; neither has published hardware specs or independent tests yet.
- **The Moxie lesson, now with the recovery path on record.** After Embodied's cloud shut down, the engineer behind OpenMoxie shipped a final robot update (24.10.803) that lets Moxie point at a custom server, plus OpenMoxie, a local Docker server that restores scheduling, missions and chat ([jbeghtol/openmoxie on GitHub](https://github.com/jbeghtol/openmoxie), accessed 2026-10-07; [Moxie overview doc](https://github.com/jbeghtol/openmoxie/blob/main/doc/MoxieOverview.md) notes the custom-endpoint setting arrived in release 24.10.801). It still needs the owner's own OpenAI key for speech-to-text and chat, because the robot's mind was built around a cloud. A figure designed mind-on-board from day one never needs that rescue.
- **An offline mind also simplifies the privacy rule.** The FTC treats internet-connected toys as online services under COPPA, and audio of a child's voice is personal information that needs verifiable parental consent ([FTC COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions), accessed 2026-10-07). The amended COPPA Rule took effect 23 Jun 2025 with compliance due 22 Apr 2026 ([Federal Register 2025-05904](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule), 22 Apr 2025). Reading of the FAQ, to confirm with counsel before sale: a figure whose voice never leaves the toy keeps children's audio out of any online service; a companion app or update channel is an online service and gets the full notice-and-consent design.
- **Toy-safety rules for a powered figure.** ASTM F963-23 is the mandatory US toy standard for toys made after 20 Apr 2024 (16 CFR part 1250; [Federal Register 2024-00741](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/01/18/2024-00741/safety-standard-mandating-astm-f963-for-toys), 18 Jan 2024; [CPSC toy safety guidance](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Toy-Safety), accessed 2026-10-07). Section 4.25 covers battery-operated toys: batteries reachable only with a common household tool for under-3 toys or small-part batteries, fasteners that stay attached to the cover, no overheating, and supplied chargers; section 8.19 tests toys with rechargeable cells; Annex A8 gives battery design guidelines ([ASTM F963-23 contents](https://store.astm.org/f0963-23.html)). A screw-closed battery door in the base, with a captive screw, meets the access rule by design.

- **A printed motor already waves an arm (2026-10-07 20:14 ET).** Georgia Tech printed stators, housings, bearings and sensing circuits for axial, radial and linear motors (magnets bought) and used them for a waving arm and a multi-legged walking robot; the axial motor reaches a 7.62 N mm/A torque constant at up to 28.2% efficiency (Schwalbe et al., "Fully 3D-Printed Wave-Wound Electromagnetic Motors", Advanced Materials Technologies, 20 Apr 2026, [doi 10.1002/admt.70994](https://doi.org/10.1002/admt.70994)). Full printing-track notes in `printing-working-machines.md`.
- **The torque a figure wave needs is small.** Derived here, with stated assumptions to check on the bench: a scaled 120 mm Motelet arm of about 5 g with its center 30 mm from the shoulder needs about 1.5 mN m to hold level (0.005 kg x 9.81 m/s2 x 0.03 m), so the 7.62 N mm/A motor does it at about 0.2 A before gearing; the 38 mm Motelet arm (about 0.3 g at 8 mm) needs about 0.02 mN m. Torque is ample. Size and heat set the design: printed coils ran at 140 C at 4.2 W in the same lab's 2023 coil actuator (IEEE/ASME AIM 2023, [doi 10.1109/AIM46323.2023.10196155](https://doi.org/10.1109/AIM46323.2023.10196155)), and the toy heat rule for a battery figure is ASTM F963 section 4.25 with its stalled-motor test (corrected 2026-10-21; section 4.4 covers 120 V toys through 16 CFR 1505), so a figure motor runs short bursts behind a current limit, a wave rather than a hold, with heat led into the base.
- **A gentle gesture fits the character.** Short waves, nods and turns are exactly what a polite, curious figure does, and they keep duty cycle low, which keeps a printed motor cool. A figure built to help and play suits this motor class well.

- **The context limit was a build choice, not a chip limit (2026-10-15 02:12 ET).** M5Stack's model list for the AX630C (Module LLM, Module LLM Kit, LLM630 Compute Kit) now includes `qwen2.5-ha-0.5b-ctx-ax630c`: a 1,024-token context window, up to 1,280 output tokens, 533.19 ms first token, 9.76 tokens/s ([M5Stack model page](https://docs.m5stack.com/en/stackflow/models/qwen2.5-ha-0.5b-instruct) and [benchmark table](https://docs.m5stack.com/en/stackflow/models/benchmark), both accessed 2026-10-15). It is fine-tuned for smart-home JSON, so it proves the chip and runtime handle 1,024 tokens at play speed rather than giving the figure its voice. Qwen3-0.6B on the same chip is listed at 128 tokens, 361.81 ms, 10.28 tokens/s ([M5Stack Qwen3-0.6B page](https://docs.m5stack.com/en/stackflow/models/qwen3-0.6b), accessed 2026-10-15). Axera's own AX630C conversion of Qwen3-VL-2B runs with a 2,047-token maximum and 1,024-token prefill groups ([AXERA-TECH on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/AXERA-TECH/Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct-GPTQ-Int4-AX630C), accessed 2026-10-15). The model weights themselves support 32,768 tokens ([Qwen3-0.6B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B)). So a general 1,024-token character build is a conversion job with Axera's toolchain.
- **A cached prefix makes memory cheap per turn (2026-10-15 02:12 ET).** Axera's ax-llm runtime (supports AX630C on SDK v3.0.0 or newer) has a multi-slot prefix cache: it stores several prompt prefixes with their attention state, matches each request by longest common prefix, prefills only the new tokens, and evicts the least recently used slot; `kv_cache_slots` turns it on ([ax-llm README](https://github.com/AXERA-TECH/ax-llm/blob/axllm/README.md), last commit 2026-09-07; [design doc docs/multi_slot_kv_cache.md](https://github.com/AXERA-TECH/ax-llm/blob/axllm/docs/multi_slot_kv_cache.md), 2026-06-12). Design that follows, to bench: the figure's character sheet plus a five-line kid memory file (about 200 tokens) is one fixed prefix, prefilled once at wake; each turn adds only what the kid said. The memory file is plain text on the module's own 32 GB eMMC, so it survives a power cycle and needs no company server, which answers the "survives the maker" half of open question 2 by design.
- **Correction (2026-10-15): Babaru no longer promises offline play.** Its maker's shop, read 2026-10-15, says setup uses a companion app and Bluetooth and states "We do not promise offline operation"; it sells for $199, ages 8 and up, with an 800 mAh 1.2 V Ni-MH cell rated 0.96 Wh ([babaru.ai shop](https://www.babaru.ai/shop), accessed 2026-10-15). The April trade announcement called it offline ([Toy World Magazine](https://toyworldmag.co.uk/pms-international-brings-babaru-to-market/), 16 Apr 2026). Derived here: 0.96 Wh would run a 1.5 W mind module for under 40 minutes before any speaker load, which is consistent with the mind living on a phone or in the cloud. That leaves a figure whose mind truly lives on board as an open position in the market.
- **Price anchors for a thinking figure (2026-10-15).** Talking AI plush: Babaru $199 ([babaru.ai](https://www.babaru.ai/), accessed 2026-10-15); Waylo Offline €199 pre-order, €349 list ([waylo.ai](https://waylo.ai/), accessed 2026-10-07); Moxie was $799 ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/08/17/1141568/moxie-when-kids-robot-best-friend-dies/), 17 Aug 2026). The mind board today retails at $99.90 as the LLM630 Compute Kit ([M5Stack store](https://shop.m5stack.com/collections/ai-hardware), accessed 2026-10-15; €70.90 at [Reichelt](https://www.reichelt.com/de/en/shop/product/m5stack_llm630_compute_kit_ax630c_-415281)). A dev kit at retail is far above a volume chip-on-board cost, so the kit price is a ceiling for the bench, and the first sellable figure aims at the $199 shelf with its mind in the base.

## Concrete experiment

Memory add-on to the bench below (no new parts): convert Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct or Qwen3-0.6B for AX630C at a 1,024-token context with Axera's ax-llm toolchain, set `kv_cache_slots` to 2, and load a character sheet plus a five-line kid memory file as the system prompt. Log first-token time on the first turn (full prefill) and on turns two to ten (prefix hit), then unplug, boot and check the greeting still uses the kid's name. Pass: first turn under 2 s, later turns under 0.6 s, memory intact.

Bench rig, about $120 in parts: an AX630C module (Module LLM or LLM630 compute kit) in a printed base box with a 2,000 mAh cell behind a captive-screw door; a scaled-up Motelet (about 120 mm, printed) holding a mic, a small speaker and two SG90-class micro servos (head turn, arm wave) on a ribbon cable. Mind script: on wake it greets the kid by a stored name and recalls last play from a 5-line memory file injected into the prompt; replies stay under 30 tokens. Log three numbers: wake-to-first-audio seconds, Wh used in 30 minutes of mixed talk and motion, and memory intact after unplugging. Pass: under 2 s, under 3 Wh, memory intact. Add-on once the printed-motor paper's full text gives sizes: weigh the printed scaled arm, hang it from a force gauge at the shoulder to confirm the derived 1.5 mN m, and log surface temperature at the shoulder over 30 minutes of waving with a thermocouple, so the printed-motor spec has a measured target.
