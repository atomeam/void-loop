The numbers

    What each line means

    Watertight
    Every edge is shared by exactly two faces, so the surface closes with no gap. A slicer needs this to know what is inside the part.
    Open edges and holes
    An edge used by one face only: the rim of a gap. The edges that join end to end make one hole; the 3D view draws them in red.
    Non-manifold edges
    An edge shared by three faces or more, as where two cubes touch along an edge. A slicer cannot tell which side is inside.
    Flipped faces
    A face wound the other way from the faces around it, so its normal points in while theirs point out. Inside-out shells are whole parts wound inward.
    Degenerate and duplicate faces
    Faces with no area, or the same three corners twice. Most slicers drop them; the mended copy does too.
    Shells and crossing faces
    Separate closed parts in one file, and faces that pass through each other (often two parts overlapping without being joined).
    Vertices and the weld
    An STL repeats each corner for every face that uses it. Corners within the weld distance are counted once, the way slicers read them.
