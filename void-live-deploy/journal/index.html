About this journal

The American Journal of Science was founded in 1818 by Benjamin Silliman and is the oldest continuously published scientific journal in the United States. It publishes peer-reviewed research on the structure, composition and evolution of the Earth and the related planetary processes, from around the world, for an international readership of researchers, educators and scientists. It is published by Yale University in New Haven, and it runs on the diamond open access model: most papers carry no publication charge at all, and only a paper that runs over the journal's standard publication-unit limits pays anything. Its print and electronic ISSNs are 0002-9599 and 1945-452X.

      How long is my paper?

      Publication units (PU) are the measure the journal's guidelines use: 1 PU for every 500 words of the text, 1.5 PU for every figure and table, the first ten equations as 1 PU and every ten more as 1 PU again, the total rounded up. A long paper (a Science Paper, a Science Review or a Technical Report) is free up to 45 PU and $100 for each PU beyond that; a short one (a Science Note, a Comment, a Reply or an Errata) may be 4 PU at most.

      For authors

      The journal's own published guidance, read on 7 November 2026 and summarised here; the rules change, so check the journal before you send anything.

      Scope

      Research that expands what is known about the Earth, with an emphasis on the structure, composition and evolution of the planet and on the physical, chemical and biological processes that shaped that evolution. Recent work covers geochemistry, geochronology, geodynamics, geomorphology, basin analysis, Earth history, petrology, tectonics and thermochronology; new directions are welcome.

      Before you submit

      • Word or PDF for the first submission, 12-point Times New Roman, 1.5 line spacing, line numbers on every page. Review is not started without numbered lines.
      • Files for review must stay under 30 MB. Large files are arranged after acceptance.
      • Abstract under 300 words and standing on its own; at least three keywords; an author-contributions statement; a data and supplementary-information statement where it applies.
      • Author affiliations and ORCID numbers, a statement of original content, and five or more suggested reviewers (and any to avoid, with a reason).
      • Figures as separate files in JPEG, TIFF or PNG at 300 dpi or better; tables as separate Word files made with the Table function; no Excel tables.

      How a paper is judged

      Relevance, generality, originality, progress, accessibility, a clear scientific foundation, integration with other fields, consistency between the results and the claims, and news value for the wider scientific community.

      The order of a manuscript

      1. Title: specific, brief, no unexplained abbreviations.
      2. Author list, then affiliations as numbered footnotes, the corresponding author marked with an asterisk.
      3. Abstract under 300 words, abbreviations defined, no reference to figures, tables or references.
      4. At least three keywords.
      5. Main text in numbered sections; three levels of heading are allowed.
      6. Acknowledgments, with the funding named.
      7. Author contributions, one line for each author.
      8. Data and supplementary information: what it is and where it lives.
      9. References for every source cited.

      Equations, tables and figures

      Equations are centred and numbered in parentheses on the right, cited as equation (1) or (eq. 1), and must come from a current Word file. A table is one file per table, built with Word's Table function, with separate cells or lists instead of spaces, tabs or line breaks. Figures are JPEG, TIFF or PNG, never vector, at least 300 dpi, one file each.

      Citations and references

      APA, 7th edition. One author: (Fisher, 1953). Two: (Dewey & Pitman, 1973). Three or more: (Davis et al., 1983). Several citations are sorted alphabetically; unpublished work is cited in the text and left out of the references. Every citation in the text must have a full reference, and every reference a citation in the text.

      Errors and statistics

      Say how each error is estimated and at what level: SE (standard error of the mean) and CI (confidence interval) are named with their level, such as 1SE, 2SE, 68% CI or 98% CI, in the same way throughout. SE is not SD: SE = SD / √n. The Greek sigma is avoided for error levels, because it is used for standard deviation.

            Policies

            Reading is free, and so is publishing within the limits

            The journal runs on the diamond open access model: no charge for readers and no charge for authors, as long as a paper stays within the standard publication-unit limits. A paper that runs over them pays $100 for each PU above the limit.

            Copyright and permissions

            Articles carry Creative Commons licences, and the licence each paper carries is shown here beside it. Permissions for older articles, reuse guidance and fair use are handled by the editorial office.

            Peer review

            Every paper is reviewed before it is published. The editors decide on the science and send the paper out; the review is blind, the reviewers are named by the author with five or more suggestions, and the corresponding author hears the decision.

            Publication ethics

            Authors confirm that the content is original, that it is not in review or in press elsewhere, and they disclose any overlap with other publications. Every listed author has agreed to the submission and has a stated contribution. Corrections and retractions come from the authors of the paper they concern.

            Conflicts of interest

            A policy for actual and perceived conflicts of interest in editorial handling, peer review and collections is still being written, and authors are asked to suggest editors who should not handle their paper, with the reason.
