ROT13 Text Scrambler

This page is for anyone who wants to shift the letters in a note, the way a puzzle or a spoiler often does, and shift them back.

The original: rot13.com

Type or paste. Pick a shift from ROT1 to ROT25. ROT13 is the one that starts out selected, and it undoes itself. Letters move and keep their case. Spaces, numbers, and other marks stay where they are. The result updates as you type. Press Rotate if scripts are off, and the same address still holds the text.

A goes to N, and Z goes to M.

How to use it

Hello, world! shifted by 13 is Uryyb, jbeyq! That pair is the one Jason McIntosh printed in 2012. Open it here: Hello, world! at ROT13. Ab! shifted by 1 is Bc! Open that too: Ab! at ROT1.

Read it back puts the result into the text box and picks the shift that returns the first text. For ROT13 that shift is still 13. For ROT5 it is ROT21. Copy result puts the result on the clipboard. A link such as the ones above opens with the text already filled in, which is the share. The links under Use this with start on Uryyb, jbeyq! until you type your own note, and then they carry the result you are looking at.

The same kind of shift is described on Wikipedia's ROT13 page, which is where the original's About link goes.

Who is said to keep it

On 23 April 2012, Jason McIntosh wrote on The Gameshelf that he had appended rot13.com to a spoiler question about the game Fez. He wrote: "The anonymous maintainers of the rot13.com website did not invent rot13; the site simply offers a simple encryption/decryption service at a pleasantly short URL." That is his description. The registry records above do not name a person. Source: Let's use rot13 for game spoilers.

The cipher is older than the site

Wikipedia describes ROT13 as a letter substitution that replaces a letter with the one 13 places later, and as a special case of the Caesar cipher. McIntosh's post says Usenet readers used that shift to mask text some people might not want to read by accident, and that news-reading software often made the rotation one command. The website is a page that does the rotation. It is not the start of the trick.

What people asked of the page

On 4 November 2011, Daniel Varga wrote on LessWrong that rot13.com was "a service frequently used here to hide spoilers." He wanted a page that updates as you type, with no submit button, that runs in the browser, and that can be opened from a link carrying the scrambled text. He also wanted something other than a substitution cipher, including for letters outside the Latin alphabet, and a password option. Source: Reforming rot13.

The live rot13.com page on 13 November 2026 does update as you type, and it offers ROT1 through ROT25, with ROT13 selected. A comment in that page's HTML points at a Stack Overflow answer as the script it uses. The page still says to turn JavaScript on. Who chose that script is not in the comment. This page keeps the letter shift, including the part Varga noted, that marks outside the alphabet stay put. It does not add a password or a different cipher.
