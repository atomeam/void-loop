A collection of translated AI research reports

Read 13 November 2026. Earliest note on that page: 17 December 2025. Newest note on that page: 26 October 2026.

What it was

A public reading list. The page title, in Chinese, means a collection of Chinese translations of AI research reports. The line under the title says it gathers translations of reports published by Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, OpenRouter, and others.

When

Who started the page is not named on the home page, and no earlier saved copy was used for this entry. On 13 November 2026 the notes were grouped by year. The newest note was dated 26 October 2026 and was an Anthropic registration statement. Notes still on that same page go back at least to 17 December 2025.

What it did

A visitor could read a translated report. One control switched the page between a light ground and a dark ground. There was no search box, no editor, and no account. The page did not change a number, a file, or a route.

An exclusive-hosting brochure

First saved home page: 5 April 2013. Read again 13 November 2026.

What it was

A one-page brochure for exclusive web hosting. The page called itself a Lianudom company. It used a stock site template: a logo, a headline, a laptop picture, and a row of icons.

When

The Internet Archive's earliest saved copy of the home page is dated 5 April 2013. Later saved copies exist for 2014, 2016, 2017, 2019, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, and 2026. Who founded the company, and whether the page has changed hands, is not in the sources read for this entry.

What it did

On 13 November 2026 the headline offered exclusive hosting. Six icons (approval, a comment, wifi, mail, share, and an announcement) each pointed at the same page. A "Chat now" link opened a Messenger thread. There was no price table, no signup form, and no calculator. A reading on 27 October 2026 had already described the page as about seventy words with those unnamed icon links and nothing a visitor could operate.

The arithmetic for bandwidth, workers, and allowed downtime is a separate Verse page, the traffic and uptime calculator. That calculator is not this brochure.

A one-page manifesto about keeping websites light

Registered 22 November 2013 at 02:09:07 UTC. First saved copy: 25 November 2013. Read again 13 November 2026.

What it was

One page of text. It argued that a good website is already simple, and that the heavy parts are things a builder adds: a hover animation a phone never sees, a library loaded only to change a colour, a font file used for a single greeting.

When

The name was registered on 22 November 2013. The registry record read on 13 November 2026 lists that registration at 02:09:07 UTC, a last change on 20 November 2025, and expiration on 22 November 2026. The registrar is NetEarth One Inc., doing business as NetEarth. The status is client transfer prohibited. The record does not name a person. The earliest saved copy opened for this entry is dated 25 November 2013, three days after registration. It already has five rules for a light page, a satire heading, a design quotation, and a comment that names a maker. The live page on 13 November 2026 still had that comment and still included a Google Analytics script.

What it did

A visitor could read the page. There was nothing to type, no form, and no account. It says it is satire, and that it is not telling you to make every site look like that one page. The design line it quotes is "Good design is as little design as possible," pointed at Vitsœ's Good design page, read on 13 November 2026, about Dieter Rams.

The count of bytes, pictures, fonts and scripts in markup you paste is a separate Verse page, the page weight tool. That count is not this manifesto.

A silent album track list

Read 9 November 2026. The page printed five tracks and start times from King Crimson's first album, In the Court of the Crimson King.

What it was

A short page that only showed a list of pieces: a start time, a title, and a length. The five titles were 21st Century Schizoid Man, I Talk to the Wind, Epitaph, Moonchild, and The Court of the Crimson King, with a total run of 43:53.

When

The page was read on 9 November 2026. Who first put the list up, and when, is not in the sources used for this entry. The list itself matches the well-known track times of that 1969 album.

What it did

A visitor could read the list. There was nothing to press, nothing to type, and nothing that played music. The page was about 1.2 KB, with no controls.

Adding up start times and the length of any list is a separate Verse page, the album track time adder. That adder is not this list.
