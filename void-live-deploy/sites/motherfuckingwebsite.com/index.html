A page can be readable text

A page can be words. This one is. It uses the headings and paragraphs a browser already knows how to show. It does not load a picture, a font file, or a script to make its point.

The words wrap on a small screen and on a large one. With every style turned off, the words are still the page. Ornament can help a person. It is also what makes a page heavy, slow, or hard to read. This is Verse's own telling of that argument.

Where that site came from

Barry T. Smith, a web designer, made motherfuckingwebsite.com. He told Digiday (2 December 2013) that the idea had been with him for a while: he would be fighting to make something work in an old browser and realise the trouble started when he added the design. He wrote the page as that argument with himself, and he said the swearing was meant to be funny because the domain was free.

Gizmodo wrote about it on 25 November 2013 and called it a single-serving site from Barry Smith, meant to show how far a simple page can go when the words are the point. The Wayback Machine saved a copy that same day.

On 10 November 2026 the live page was still that essay: headings and paragraphs arguing that a site should load fast, fit any screen, work in any browser, and stay readable, and that extra decoration is what breaks those things. The essay there is his, and this page does not repeat it. The job is the same: a page a person can read, and nothing added for show.

Sites are named in plain text. Verse is not them.
