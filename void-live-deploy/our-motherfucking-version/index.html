Lightweight Page

This page is for anyone who wants a page that stays readable, and a way to weigh how much markup another page is carrying.

The original: motherfuckingwebsite.com

The original is one page of text. It argues that a good website is already simple, and that the heavy parts are things a builder adds. These are the points it makes, in this page's own words.

What it asks a page to be

  • Light, so it loads quickly.
  • Able to fit a small screen and a large one.
  • Readable in more than one browser.
  • Usable by people who do not see the pictures, including people who use a screen reader.
  • Clear enough that the sentence is the point, not a large photograph.

Where the weight comes from

A hover animation on a banner can cost tens of kilobytes, and a person tapping a phone never sees that hover. A library loaded only to change a background color, or a whole family of font files loaded so one greeting can be huge, is weight the sentence did not need. The original says its own page is lighter than a typical logo or a font stack used for a single hello.

It fits the screen without a special query

The original says a page can respond to the screen by letting the text reflow. It does not use a media query. The words wrap on a wide monitor and on a tiny screen. That is the whole trick.

You can read it

The original uses headings, a list, a quotation, and HTML5 elements so the structure is in the markup, not only in a picture. The words are on the first screen. They are not stretched across several empty screens behind a button whose only job is to scroll.

It also says to open the page in a very old browser. The claim is that plain HTML still shows the words there.

It says this is satire

The original says it is not telling you to make every site look like that one page. It says websites are already functional, fast, and accessible, and that the problems are ones builders create. The line it quotes is "Good design is as little design as possible." It points that line at Vitsœ's Good design page. That page, read on 13 November 2026, is about Dieter Rams. The original does not print his name.
